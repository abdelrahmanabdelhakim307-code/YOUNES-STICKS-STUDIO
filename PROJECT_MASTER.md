# YOUNES STICKS STUDIO — Project Master

## Mission
Build a repeatable, beginner-friendly production studio for the YOUNES STICKS animated stickman channel. The studio turns an idea into a complete, consistent episode package: concept, script, storyboard, shot prompts, voiceover, edit plan, and publishing assets.

## Character continuity
Use the approved Younes reference image as the single source of truth. Preserve the character's silhouette, head and hair, cap, outfit, proportions, colors, and signature details across every shot. Do not redesign the character between scenes. When a reference image is supplied, use it consistently for image and video generation.

## Visual identity
- Channel wordmark: YOUNES in the approved graffiti treatment.
- Supporting descriptor: STICKS.
- Keep the approved logo treatment intact; do not invent a replacement logo.
- Use clean, readable layouts and a consistent visual system across thumbnails, episode cards, and documents.
- Keep all character clothing and props consistent unless an episode brief explicitly approves a change.

## Content principles
- Make stories understandable without complex dialogue.
- Use clear visual storytelling, expressive poses, readable staging, and purposeful camera framing.
- Favor relatable everyday situations, humor, curiosity, small surprises, and positive takeaways.
- Avoid copying existing creators, characters, scripts, or recognizable protected designs.
- Keep content suitable for a broad audience.

## Standard production pipeline
1. Idea and audience promise.
2. Logline and story beats.
3. Full script with dialogue, action, and timing.
4. Storyboard with numbered shots and camera directions.
5. Character and background continuity checklist.
6. Per-shot image/video generation prompts.
7. Arabic voiceover script and delivery notes.
8. Editing timeline, sound design, captions, and transitions.
9. Thumbnail concept, title, description, and tags.
10. Quality-control review and export checklist.

## Episode deliverables
Every episode should include:
- A one-sentence premise and intended audience.
- Runtime target and emotional tone.
- Beginning, middle, ending, and final payoff.
- Scene-by-scene storyboard.
- Shot list with framing, movement, action, setting, and continuity notes.
- Prompts that identify the character reference and specify only the changes needed in that shot.
- Arabic voiceover/dialogue, with pronunciation notes where needed.
- Editing and audio notes.
- Publishing package.

## Production rules
- Never assume a generated shot matches the reference; review it.
- Keep prompts specific, concise, and shot-focused.
- Generate shots separately when consistency or control matters.
- Do not add text inside generated images unless explicitly requested; add titles and captions during editing.
- If a tool or model is unavailable, provide a practical alternative and preserve the workflow.
- Prefer free tools and workflows first; clearly flag paid features before recommending them.
